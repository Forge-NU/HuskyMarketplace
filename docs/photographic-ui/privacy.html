Husky Marketplace — Privacy Policy

DRAFT FOR REVIEW • Prepared October 11, 2026 • NOT FOR PUBLIC LAUNCH WITHOUT REVISION

1. Scope

This draft describes the intended handling of personal information when students use the Husky Marketplace website to discover listings, contact other students, and coordinate moving help. The operator must revise this notice to match the actual implemented code and service providers before launch. Husky is not a payment processor or escrow service.

2. Information we collect

We plan to collect account details (display name, verified school email, authentication status and relevant security events), optional profile information, general campus pickup area, listing titles, prices, descriptions and photos, messages, moving-help requests, saved items, searches, browsing events, preferences, support reports, and basic device/browser event logs necessary for security and site operations. Do not include exact dorm room addresses, card data, or bank login details in public listings or messages. If Microsoft account login is implemented, Microsoft may supply limited profile and authentication claims under the permissions approved by the university and user.

3. Why we use information

To create and verify accounts, operate listings and messaging, help members coordinate, respond to abuse reports, secure the service, remember preferences, measure site performance, and surface relevant campus listings. Personalized recommendations may use search terms, viewed categories, saved listings, price interests, general campus area, listing availability, and recency. Users should be able to switch to "Newest" browsing and modify or turn off personalization without losing marketplace access. We do not plan to infer sensitive traits or track precise location for recommendations.

4. Essential vs. optional emails

Account-verification, password-reset, service, legal, and relevant safety notifications may be sent when necessary. Emails containing new-arrival suggestions, personalized listing picks, weekly digests, or promotional updates are optional and turned off by default. A separate signup checkbox permits opting in. Users can adjust frequency (off, weekly, daily, or immediate alerts if supported), category topics, and unsubscribe anytime. Each promotional email should have compliant unsubscribe instructions, truthful sender information, and the operator's valid postal address; the system must honor opt-outs promptly.

5. Who sees information

Verified members may see public profiles, general pickup areas, listings, and the content members choose to post. Conversation participants can see their messages, subject to necessary moderation, safety enforcement, and legal compliance. Service providers may receive information needed for hosting, authentication, email delivery, analytics, image storage, support, or security, subject to contracts and safeguards. We do not plan to sell students' personal information. Exact vendors and any disclosure categories must be filled in before launch.

6. Retention and deletion

Retention schedules, deletion workflows, backups, fraud-prevention exceptions, and the method for requesting access or deletion must be defined and disclosed before launch. Users should be able to delete saved interests, adjust recommendations, stop marketing emails, and request account closure. Some records may need to be retained for legitimate security, legal, or abuse-prevention reasons.

7. Cookies, storage, analytics, and security

The live service may use necessary session cookies and browser storage to maintain login and preferences. Any advertising or optional analytics cookies require appropriate notice and controls based on applicable law. Use encryption in transit, access restrictions, and other reasonable safeguards. Student email verification is not an identity or background check.

8. Minors, international users, and rights

The initial product is intended for users 18 or older. The operator must establish procedures for underage accounts and applicable privacy requests, including jurisdiction-specific rights and appeals where required. Third-party services such as Microsoft, Zelle, PayPal, Venmo, or email vendors apply their own privacy terms to their services.

9. Changes and contact

Publish a dated notice and provide material-change notifications when required. Direct privacy questions or deletion requests to [PRIVACY CONTACT].

Important: This is a product-policy draft describing intended practices. Audit actual data flows, email tools, analytics SDKs, logging, third-party integrations, and local laws before it becomes a public Privacy Policy.
